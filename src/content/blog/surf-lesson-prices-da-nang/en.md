Ask what surf lessons cost in Da Nang and you'll get three different numbers from three different tabs: $8 on a booking platform, $44 on an affiliate page, somewhere between $23 and $35 in the ranges the local price guides publish. All three are "real". None of them are comparing the same thing. This page is about what the money actually buys — the board, the time, the coach, the photos — so you can pick a lesson instead of just a price. If you want the beach itself explained (season, forecast, which peak to paddle out on), that's in our [My Khe surf guide](/blog/my-khe-beach-surf-guide/); this page sticks to the money question.

## What a surf lesson in Da Nang actually costs

![Early morning surf check — the conditions your booked lesson is gambling on](/images/blog/surf-lesson-prices-da-nang/early-morning-surf-beach.jpg)
<sub>Photo: Early morning beach and surf in Ocean City, MD · the bridge · CC BY 2.0</sub>

Start with what's publicly out there, because it's the honest context for every price you'll see. The published Da Nang price guides put a guided lesson with a board at roughly 600,000–900,000 VND for about 90 minutes — call it $23–35. Booking platforms advertise lesson-and-board packages from around $8–11, and affiliate roundups headline "from $44" for a two-hour intro. Board rental on its own, if you're pricing the parts, runs somewhere between 100,000 and 300,000 VND an hour, or roughly 300,000–600,000 VND for a full day depending on who's counting.

Two honest caveats before you anchor on any of those numbers. First: these are market ranges, not quotes. Nobody in Da Nang publishes a per-school price list — the guides that publish prices at all talk about activity types, not shops — and we're not going to invent one. Second: the range is wide because the products inside it are different. A $9 platform package and a $34 walk-up lesson with a local instructor are not the same hour, and section three below explains exactly where the money goes.

For completeness, our own number, verified on our booking page: a surf lesson with Nhi Local is **$65 for 60 minutes at My An**, board, leash, coaching, and photo/video included. Yes, that's above the published local range. The rest of this page is the honest accounting of why — and a checklist for judging every other offer on the beach by the same standard.

## What the price is hiding: 5 things to check before you book

![A rack of boards at a surfboard rental shop — the gear a lesson may or may not include](/images/blog/surf-lesson-prices-da-nang/surfboard-rental-shop.jpg)
<sub>Photo: Surfboard Rental Shop · Visitor7 · CC BY-SA 3.0</sub>

Two lessons can both say "surf lesson, Da Nang, beginner welcome" and still differ by 3× in what you actually get. Before you book anything, at any shop or platform, get answers to these five.

**1. Is the board (and leash) included — or billed separately?** This is the single biggest swing in what a lesson costs you, and it's almost never stated. We went through the pages that publish Da Nang surf prices and not one of them says whether the board is part of the price. If the lesson doesn't include a board, you're really paying lesson-plus-rental — add 100,000–300,000 VND an hour to any quoted number and compare again.

**2. How long is the session — and how much of it is in the water?** "90 minutes" that includes 30 minutes of sand instruction and 15 minutes of photos can mean less water time than a tight 60. There's no correct answer — a first-timer genuinely needs the beach briefing — but you should know which product you're buying.

**3. How many people per instructor?** Beginner lessons live or die on this. Past a handful of learners per coach, most of your session is spent waiting for a correction that arrives late, or catching nothing because the coach is two peaks away helping someone else. Small groups cost more to run. That's not a markup; it's the product.

**4. Do you get photo or video of your session?** For a beginner this isn't vanity — it's the fastest way to actually improve between sessions. What you think you're doing on a wave and what the footage shows are rarely the same thing. Some lessons include it, some sell it as an add-on, most don't offer it at all. Worth knowing before, not after.

**5. Is the instructor English-speaking, and is the time fixed?** A lesson you booked for 7am that the vendor slides to "come back around 9" is a different lesson — surf conditions at 9 in Da Nang are often windier and more crowded. A fixed, confirmable time and an instructor who can actually coach you in a language you speak are both part of what a price buys.

Ask those five questions of any offer and the prices stop being comparable-by-number and start being comparable-by-content. Which brings us to the odd part of this market.

## Why the same lesson is $11 on one page and $44 on another

![A group surf lesson on the sand — group size is one of the biggest price drivers](/images/blog/surf-lesson-prices-da-nang/group-surf-lesson-beach.jpg)
<sub>Photo: Surfing Lesson · Tobyotter · CC BY 2.0</sub>

The same search can show you an $11 surf lesson and a $44 surf lesson, and neither number is a scam. They're different products that sound identical. Here's what actually moves the price:

- **Platform commission.** Booking sites take a cut — commonly 20–30% — baked into the price you see. The $11 lesson may mean $8 to the school and change to the platform. Cheap platform listings are often loss-leaders: the school earns almost nothing on them and runs them accordingly, with big groups and quick turnarounds.
- **Group vs private.** A private lesson can cost 2–3× a group slot for the same duration. Some listings don't say which one you're buying until checkout.
- **Duration and water time.** 60 minutes and 90 minutes are both sold as "a lesson". So are sessions where half the time is transport and setup.
- **Gear.** Board and leash included is a cost the school carries; board-as-extra moves it to you.
- **What happens after.** Photo/video, feedback, a debrief — included, paid extra, or nonexistent.

None of these drivers is invisible once you know to look. The $44 two-hour intro and the $23–35 local range and our own $65 are all just the same five dials set differently. Read the dials and you can book anything on this beach with a clear idea of what you bought.

## Booking in advance: when it matters

![Surfers walking down to the water with their boards for a booked morning session](/images/blog/surf-lesson-prices-da-nang/surfers-walking-beach-boards.jpg)
<sub>Photo: Father and Son, down steps to water winter, with surf boards · Wonderlane · CC BY 2.0</sub>

Most of the beach runs on walk-up logic: show up, rent, paddle out. Lessons are the exception, and it's worth understanding why. A lesson needs an instructor, a board, and a slot of undivided time — and small groups mean a morning only fits so many learners. Book the day before (or the morning of, early) and you're choosing your window; show up cold at 10am in peak season and the choices get thin fast.

Season matters more than the calendar in general. Da Nang's surf window is roughly October through February, when the monsoon pushes swell onto this coast; the exact month-by-month and how to read a forecast for My Khe live in [the guide](/blog/my-khe-beach-surf-guide/), so we won't repeat them here. Inside that window, book ahead — the good mornings fill. Outside it, lessons still run on the smaller summer days, but there's less to be full, and same-day is usually fine.

## FAQ

![A surf school session getting ready on the sand](/images/blog/surf-lesson-prices-da-nang/surf-school-beach.jpg)
<sub>Photo: Surf School · glennharper · CC BY 2.0</sub>

**How much is a surf lesson in Da Nang?**
Published local ranges put a guided lesson with a board at roughly $23–35 (about 600,000–900,000 VND) for ~90 minutes, with platform packages advertised from around $8–11 and some two-hour intros headlined at $44 and up. What any of those includes varies more than the prices do — check the board, the group size, and the duration before comparing. Nhi Local's lesson is $65 for 60 minutes with board, leash, coaching, and photo/video included.

**Is surfing expensive in Vietnam?**
No — it's one of the cheaper places in Asia to get into the water. Board rental runs on the order of 100,000–300,000 VND an hour, and even the upper end of the lesson range is modest by, say, Bali or Australia standards. The costs that surprise people are the add-ons: board-not-included lessons, photo packages, transport.

**Do surf lessons include the board?**
Some do, some don't — and listings rarely say. Ask directly. If the board is extra, add the going rental rate (100,000–300,000 VND an hour) to the advertised price before you compare. Nhi Local's lesson includes the board and the leash; it's part of the price, not an add-on.

**Do I need to book a surf lesson in advance?**
During surf season (October–February), yes — small-group lessons fill and mornings go first. Off-season, same-day usually works. Rentals, as opposed to lessons, are generally walk-up friendly year-round.
